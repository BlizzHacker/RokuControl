Privacy Policy for Open Remote Control

Last updated: November 2026

Open Remote Control does not collect, store on any server, share, or transmit personal information, usage data, or telemetry.

The app communicates directly with Roku devices and Android TV / Google TV devices on your local network. Roku control uses the Roku External Control Protocol (ECP) on port 8060 and local SSDP discovery. Android TV pairing and control use Android TV Remote Service on the local network (pairing port 6467 and control port 6466); the TV displays a code that you enter to authorize this app. No commands are sent to an external server. Roku Wake-on-LAN packets, when used, also stay on your local network.

Stored only on your computer: Roku and Android TV device names, IP addresses, models and Roku network hardware (MAC) addresses; app window preferences; usage counters used only to show a one-time invitation to rate the app; and the Android TV pairing certificate and private key. The pairing key is kept in the app's local application-data folder so the TV can recognize this installation after restart. The key is never sent anywhere except to the Android TV device as part of local TLS pairing and control. Forgetting a TV removes its saved device entry; to revoke a paired certificate, remove Open Remote Control from the TV's Android TV Remote Service paired-device settings.

Links in the app open your browser or the Microsoft Store only when clicked. A GitHub bug-report link may include the app version, operating system, selected TV family and model, and a redacted error. It never includes an IP address, device name, serial number, MAC address or pairing key. Nothing is sent unless you submit it on GitHub.

This app does not collect personal information, use analytics or crash reporting, contain advertisements, or require an account or login. It does not access the internet on its own; it talks to selected TVs on your LAN.
